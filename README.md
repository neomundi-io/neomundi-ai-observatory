# NeoMundi AI Observatory

## Measuring AI behaviour over time

The NeoMundi AI Observatory is an open research initiative dedicated to observing, measuring and documenting how AI systems behave across repeated and comparable conditions.

It produces longitudinal measurements, public methodologies, datasets, analytical releases and reproducible evidence related to:

- stability;
- semantic variation;
- behavioural drift;
- factual-risk signals;
- coherence;
- cost and token consumption;
- latency;
- measurement coverage;
- observation conditions and limitations.

NeoMundi does not publish rankings, universal verdicts or model certifications.

Its purpose is to produce measurements, traces, methods and public evidence that can be examined, challenged, improved and reused.

> **A signal is not a verdict. Observation must remain distinguishable from interpretation.**

## Mission

AI systems are increasingly used in consequential environments, yet their behaviour may change silently across time, providers, prompts, policies, configurations, infrastructure or deployment conditions.

The NeoMundi AI Observatory exists to help answer operational questions:

- What was observed?
- Under which conditions was it observed?
- How stable or variable was the behaviour?
- Did the system change over time?
- Which signals can be measured?
- Which conclusions require contextual interpretation?
- What evidence can support governance, audit and operational decision-making?

The Observatory treats AI behaviour as an object of continuous measurement rather than as a fixed property inferred from a single benchmark.

---

## Measurement principles

The Observatory follows several core principles:

- **Repeated observation** rather than one-time evaluation;
- **Longitudinal measurement** rather than static snapshots;
- **Comparable conditions** wherever technically possible;
- **Transparent methodologies and limitations**;
- **Separation of measurement from interpretation**;
- **De-identification of observed systems in public releases**;
- **No public leaderboard by default**;
- **Public evidence without disclosure of protected operational data**;
- **Reproducibility within explicitly documented boundaries**.

Observed signals may indicate variation, instability, factual risk or regime change. They do not independently establish overall model quality, safety, compliance or suitability for a specific use case.

## Research programmes

The Observatory currently organises its public measurement work around two recurring programmes.

### Weekly AI Barometer

The Weekly AI Barometer conducts repeated measurements designed to detect behavioural variation and drift over time.

It observes whether AI systems:

- remain stable across repeated executions;
- change response regime;
- exhibit semantic variation;
- produce factual-risk signals;
- show changes in measurement coverage;
- behave differently from one observation period to another.

**Repository:**  
[NeoMundi Weekly AI Barometer](https://github.com/neomundi-io/NeoMundi-Weekly-Barometer)

The repository includes:

- the public methodology;
- the reference baseline;
- weekly public releases;
- aggregated datasets;
- metric definitions;
- interpretation boundaries;
- release manifests and provenance information.

---

### Monthly AI Behaviour Cartography

The Monthly AI Behaviour Cartography conducts comparative measurement campaigns across shared or documented observation conditions.

It is designed to map differences among AI systems without reducing the results to a single score or ranking.

The programme may include measurements related to:

- stability;
- semantic variation;
- factual validity;
- agreement between evaluation methods;
- coherence;
- cost;
- token consumption;
- latency;
- behavioural regimes;
- response structure;
- measurement limits.

**Repository:**  
[NeoMundi AI Behaviour Cartography](https://github.com/neomundi-io/ai-behavior-cartography)

The Cartography provides a comparative view, while the Weekly Barometer provides temporal monitoring. Together, they support both cross-system and longitudinal observation.

## Observation architecture

The Observatory progressively structures its public work around five complementary functions:

1. **Runtime observation**  
   Capture AI behaviour under documented execution conditions.

2. **Behavioural signal production**  
   Measure stability, variation, coherence, factual-risk signals, latency, cost and behavioural regimes.

3. **Longitudinal analysis**  
   Compare measurements across repeated executions and observation periods.

4. **Public evidence production**  
   Publish aggregated data, methodologies, manifests, analytical reports and reproducibility information.

5. **Governance interoperability**  
   Make runtime measurements usable by audit, compliance, observability, governance and execution-control systems.

The Observatory produces evidence and signals. Governance or execution decisions remain the responsibility of the systems and organisations that consume them.

## Progressive research tracks

Additional research tracks may be introduced as the Observatory, datasets and methodologies mature.

### Weekly topical questions

Recurring public questions linked to current events, designed to observe how AI systems respond to recent, socially relevant or time-sensitive subjects.

### Intra-provider measurements

Repeated measurements within the same provider or model family, designed to observe internal variability, consistency, drift and regime changes.

### Vertical-sector measurements

Domain-specific observation programmes for sectors such as:

- law;
- healthcare;
- finance;
- insurance;
- education;
- public administration;
- cybersecurity;
- industrial systems.

### External reviews and independent analyses

Methodological reviews, contributor-led analyses, external audits and public commentary on released results.

### Experimental protocols

New observation formats, datasets and analytical methods introduced under clearly documented experimental conditions.

These tracks must not be interpreted as certifications, rankings or final conclusions unless an explicit methodology states otherwise.

## Public evidence and reproducibility

The Observatory publishes public evidence through dedicated repositories and official publications.

Depending on the programme, public artefacts may include:

- aggregated datasets;
- release summaries;
- measurement contracts;
- methodology documents;
- baseline campaigns;
- file manifests;
- integrity hashes;
- analytical reports;
- visual cartographies;
- longitudinal comparisons;
- documented limitations.

Public releases are designed to support:

- internal consistency checks;
- methodological review;
- independent analysis;
- comparison across releases;
- public discussion of observed signals.

Full reproduction from source may require access to protected campaign exports, infrastructure configuration or restricted operational data.

Each repository documents its own reproducibility boundary.

## Data protection and de-identification

Public Observatory releases use aggregated and de-identified information by default.

Observed systems may be represented through stable opaque profile identifiers. Public identifiers are not assigned according to performance, provider name, model name, alphabetical order, score or rank.

Certain information may remain restricted, including:

- provider and model mappings;
- prompts or prompt identifiers;
- complete model responses;
- request and trace identifiers;
- raw payloads;
- per-response timestamps;
- API keys and credentials;
- detailed private costs;
- unpublished results;
- debugging information;
- strategic or commercially sensitive material.

The existence of a private mapping registry means that public releases must be described as **de-identified**, rather than as irreversibly anonymous.

Access to non-public material is not automatic and may require a specific governance, research or confidentiality framework.

## Experimental international stations

NeoMundi is preparing a distributed network of experimental measurement and applied-research stations.

These stations are intended to:

- conduct local or regional measurement campaigns;
- reproduce shared protocols;
- test independent articulations;
- document context-specific use cases;
- contribute observations and analyses to the common corpus;
- connect local infrastructures, researchers and institutions;
- strengthen the geographical and institutional diversity of the Observatory.

During the experimental phase, a station is:

- a contribution and measurement node;
- not a subsidiary;
- not a franchise;
- not an autonomous legal entity;
- not an exclusive commercial representative;
- not authorised to legally bind NeoMundi.

Station autonomy is expected to grow progressively through measurement quality, methodological rigour, continuity of contribution, real-world use and accumulated trust.

Individual station profiles will be published after contributor validation and formal acceptance of the experimental framework.

**Framework repository:**  
[NeoMundi Distributed Stations](https://github.com/neomundi-io/neomundi-distributed-stations)

## Institutional and standards infrastructure

NeoMundi is progressively developing the institutional, governance and standardisation foundations required to support independent AI metrology over the long term.

This work is intended to document and strengthen:

- the institutional architecture of the Observatory;
- scientific and methodological independence;
- stewardship of public evidence;
- contribution and attribution rules;
- conflict-of-interest management;
- methodological revision processes;
- publication and archival doctrine;
- intellectual-property and licensing principles;
- interoperability requirements;
- relationships with external governance systems;
- the path toward international standardisation of runtime AI metrology.

**Public repository:**  
[NeoMundi Distributed Stations — Governance, Data and Contribution Framework](https://github.com/neomundi-io/neomundi-distributed-stations)

The repository documents the experimental governance, data-stewardship, contribution, attribution and progressive-autonomy framework for NeoMundi’s distributed international stations.

It also defines the default principles governing:

- raw-data preservation;
- shared collaborative workspaces;
- campaign manifests;
- component provenance;
- correction history;
- publication approval;
- station reversibility;
- commercial opportunities;
- the relationship between local stations and the NeoMundi Hub.

The institutional programme develops alongside the public measurement programmes, but at a different pace. Public measurements establish empirical legitimacy; institutional infrastructure supports continuity, accountability and future standardisation.

## Contribution principles

Ideas travel.  
Formalised contributions are attributed.  
Signed texts are respected.  
Sensitive information is protected.  
Published archives become stable.

The Observatory is designed to encourage:

- cooperation without extraction;
- attribution without ownership capture;
- independent review without institutional dependency;
- measurement without proclamation;
- transparency without uncontrolled disclosure.
